SELECTED RESEARCH  
Surface Compliance, Silent Resistance 
Real-World Workplace Ethnography  ·  Independent Behavioral Research  ·  Jan–Aug 2026  ·  Two-Part Study 
Research question: When management introduces control-based policies in response to a perceived productivity problem, what 
behavioral patterns emerge in the affected team, and how does that pattern evolve as management escalates from control 
toward personnel removal? 
• 6-month embedded ethnographic observation of a 7-person software team, conducted as an active insider with real-time 
access to policy rollout and management interactions; cross-validated against a natural internal control group and a cross-
team bridge role. 
• Developed the Control–Trust–Output Loop, a behavioral model showing how management's structural control policies were 
read as distrust signals — decreasing discretionary effort and output, and prompting further controls that reproduced the 
original problem. 
• Synthesized field evidence into 3 testable hypotheses and 3 proposed interventions (a requirements-alignment discovery 
sprint, embedding a developer in client deadline discussions, and replacing presence tracking with outcome metrics). 
• Part 2 — The Exit Arc (Jul–Aug 2026): tracked the loop into a terminal phase as management’s controls escalated from 
policy into personnel-level consequences, including the observer’s own exit — captured as reflexive data rather than left out 
of the analysis. 
• Identified a structural mirror: management's own flight from direct client accountability matched the same avoidance 
behavior it had been punishing in employees. 
• Extended the Control–Trust–Output Loop into an 11-stage terminal model and proposed 3 crisis-stage interventions to 
replace scapegoating with root-cause discovery.
